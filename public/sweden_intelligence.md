**Outlet:** TV4 Nyheterna
**Category:** General News | **Date:** 2026-06-21 (today)

Now the German government wants to clean up and build new shelters. A huge challenge in a country with over 80 million inhabitants. At the Gesundbrunnen train station in northern Berlin there is a small white house. If you unlock the door and go in, there is a staircase there. If you follow it, you will come to the German capital's only shelter. And it's not impressive.  Around 900 people can fit. In the largest room there are some wooden benches. In another room there is a stack of folding chairs and cans for water.  The funding was withdrawn. The shelter was built in the 1920s and was used extensively during the Second World War. After the war, it and other similar shelters were maintained in West Berlin. The threat from East Germany and the Soviet Union was palpable for decades. However, with the fall of the Berlin Wall, political interest in the shelters disappeared, and in the mid-2000s, funding was completely withdrawn for all shelters throughout the country.  This very thing was preserved as a museum object, and the organization Berlins Underjord makes small guided walks through the facility. And should there be a crisis or a war, the organization will open the shelter to the public.  But for a city of 3.8 million people, Berlin's only preserved shelter is of course a drop in the ocean.  The government wants to invest All around Germany it looks the same. A few shelters have been preserved for nostalgic reasons and some private individuals have built their own bunkers. But in the event of a war or a crisis, there is only room for less than one percent of the population.  The German government has now put forward a proposal to clean up shelters and build new ones and will soon release an app that shows the nearest place where people can seek shelter in a crisis. The concern that Kay Heyne has is that too many resources are being put into the app and not enough into the shelters.  - Right now the app would only show that there are no shelters at all, he says and laughs.

**Category:** Central Bank | **Date:** 2026-06-17 (4 days old)

Inflation in Sweden is low and the economic situation is somewhat weaker than normal. At the same time, the supply disruptions linked to the war in the Middle East have led to increased inflationary pressure and the risks of inflation becoming too high have increased. The management assesses that it is well balanced to now leave the key interest rate unchanged at 1.75 percent, but the probability that the interest rate will be raised later this year has increased compared to the assessment in March.
