## El Niño is growing unusually strongly - could be the strongest yet
**Outlet:** TV4 Nyheterna
**Category:** General News | **Date:** 2026-08-22 (today)

The weather pattern already appears to be affected by El Niño, writes the BBC. India has had weaker than normal monsoon rains and the Atlantic has had a subdued hurricane season so far. - I have never seen an El Niño signal this strong in our forecasts, says Adam Scaife at the Met Office to the channel. The weather phenomenon El Niño occurs when the sea surface temperature in a key area of ​​the Pacific Ocean is at least 0.5 degrees above the long-term average. Now the deviation is over two degrees. The British weather service Met Office estimates that warming could exceed 3 degrees when the event reaches its peak later this year - something unprecedented since measurements began in 1950 and probably the strongest since the 19th century, according to the BBC.  Very high temperatures in deeper water can further enhance development. In some places, the temperature is around 8 degrees above normal at a depth of 100 metres. Climate change exacerbates the effects There is no clear evidence that climate change is making El Niño more common. But in a warmer world, the weather effects are likely to be more extreme, according to the researchers. In Great Britain, long-term forecasts point to an increased risk of a wet and stormy autumn. Coupled with human-caused warming, the Met Office warns that 2027 is very likely to set a new global heat record. Risk of record levels Other research groups have models that point to even stronger warming, up to 4 degrees above normal levels in the Pacific Ocean.  Should that happen, scientists estimate that El Niño could be the strongest in 500-1,000 years, but the uncertainty is great far back in time, according to the BBC. - We have many months left while the event continues to develop, and that is one of the reasons why we are more confident that this will be a record event, says Zeke Hausfather of Berkeley Earth to the BBC.
