The penalty is 14 years in prison.  The district court writes that "no evidence taken by itself is such that it is proven that the man is the perpetrator, but the collected evidence is such that he has in any case been guilty of aiding and abetting murder. Considering that the two victims were killed in the same place, at the same time and in the same way, the district court considers that the man was guilty of aiding and abetting both murders. The penalty is set at 14 years' imprisonment, which was the strictest fixed-term penalty which could be sentenced in 2005.” Must pay damages Johan Jacobsson must also pay approximately SEK 250,000 in damages to the next of kin and SEK 120,000 to the state for criminal damage compensation paid. - The investigation has been extensive and the collected evidence means that there is no other reasonable explanation for the defendant's DNA being found on one of the victims than that he was involved in both murders, says Anita Enocksson, former councilor in the district court's press release. The evidence After a breakthrough in the investigation in the spring, DNA genealogy was able to link Johan Jacobsson to a DNA trace that had been secured on Gerd Viklund's ankle. He was arrested and remanded in custody in April. According to the indictment, Johan Jacobsson, by himself or together and in agreement with another person or persons, took the lives of Tor Öberg and Gerd Viklund by several blows to the head with an attachment. Johan Jacobsson has always denied murder, but believes that he handled Gerd's body. Has provided different information During the trial, Johan Jacobsson told his version of what happened.
